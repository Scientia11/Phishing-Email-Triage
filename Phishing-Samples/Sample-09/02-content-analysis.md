## Analysis

1. Brand Impersonation

The email impersonates **Norton**, a well-known cybersecurity company, by referencing Norton products and Norton LifeLock.


2. Generic Greeting

The message begins with:
```text
 Dear Member,
```
Legitimate Norton communications generally personalize billing emails.


3. False Subscription Renewal

The phrase "electronic consent" is vague and unusual for a legitimate billing confirmation. Official subscription notices typically reference automatic renewal settings rather than ambiguous legal terminology.


4. Fear: the Large unauthorized charge is an attempt to make the recipient panic upon seeing the unexpected amount and immediately contact the listed phone number.


5. Fake Billing Information

The email includes several details intended to make the message appear authentic credible:

* Billing ID
* Renewal date
* Activation key
* Tax amount
* Total amount
* Upcoming renewal date



 6. Telephone callback request

Instead of directing users to log into their Norton account or visit the official website, the attacker encourages direct phone contact.


7. Missing Corporate Elements

Several features commonly found in legitimate Norton billing emails are absent:

* Official Norton logo
* Company address
* Customer account information
* Subscription management portal
* Secure customer login link
* Purchase confirmation reference
* Official support links

The lack of complete corporate branding reduces the credibility of the message.
